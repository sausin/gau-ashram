# Water Conservation Success: From 410ft to 120ft

When we first arrived at our Go Mandir campus, the land was parched, and the water table had dropped to an alarming 410 feet. Local farmers were struggling with bore wells that had run dry, and many had given up hope of reviving their agricultural practices.

## The Challenge

The region had suffered from:
- Decades of over-extraction of groundwater
- Loss of traditional water harvesting structures
- Deforestation leading to reduced water retention
- Chemical farming practices that degraded soil water-holding capacity

## Our Approach

We implemented a comprehensive water conservation strategy based on traditional wisdom:

### 1. Contour Trenching
We created contour trenches across the landscape to capture rainwater runoff and allow it to percolate into the ground slowly.

### 2. Check Dams
Small check dams were built across seasonal streams to slow water flow and increase infiltration.

### 3. Percolation Ponds
We constructed several percolation ponds at strategic locations to collect rainwater and recharge groundwater.

### 4. Afforestation
Massive tree plantation drives were undertaken with native species that help in water retention and improve the microclimate.

### 5. Organic Farming
Transitioning to organic farming improved soil structure and its water-holding capacity.

## The Results

After five years of consistent effort:
- Water table rose from 410 feet to 120 feet
- Dry bore wells started yielding water again
- Agricultural productivity increased by 40%
- Biodiversity returned to the region
- Microclimate improved significantly

## Lessons Learned

This transformation taught us that:
1. Traditional water harvesting methods are still the most effective
2. Community participation is crucial for success
3. Nature heals quickly when given a chance
4. Sustainable practices create lasting change

Today, our campus serves as a model for water conservation, inspiring neighboring villages to adopt similar practices. The sound of water in once-dry wells is a testament to the power of traditional wisdom combined with community action.
